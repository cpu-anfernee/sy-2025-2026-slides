Database Normalization

Exercises (Chapters 7.1 - 7.3)

SE 2141 Database Systems

Sample 1: Library Loans

Consider the following schema designed to track current library book loans:

Loan(PatronID, BookID, DueDate, PatronName)

Business Rules:

  • A book can only be loaned to one patron at a time, with a specific due date.
  • Each patron has a single registered name.

Task: Identify the functional dependencies, determine if the schema is in BCNF, and perform a lossless decomposition if it is not.

Sample 1: Solution

Functional Dependencies:

  • BookID → PatronID, DueDate
  • PatronID → PatronName

Analysis: BookID is the primary key. The schema violates BCNF because PatronID → PatronName is a non-trivial dependency where the determinant (PatronID) is not a superkey.

Decomposition:

  • Patron(PatronID, PatronName)
  • Loan(BookID, PatronID, DueDate)

Sample 2: Consulting Assignments

Consider a schema for assigning consultants to client projects:

Assignment(Consultant, Client, ProjectManager)

Business Rules:

  • A consultant works for a specific client under the direction of exactly one project manager.
  • Each project manager is assigned to exactly one client, but can manage multiple consultants.

Task: Identify the FDs. Is this schema in 3NF? Is it in BCNF? Decompose to BCNF if it is not.

Sample 2: Solution

Functional Dependencies:

  • Consultant, Client → ProjectManager
  • ProjectManager → Client

Analysis: The candidate keys are {Consultant, Client} and {Consultant, ProjectManager}.

3NF: Yes. While ProjectManager is not a superkey, Client is a prime attribute (part of a candidate key).
BCNF: No. ProjectManager → Client violates BCNF because ProjectManager is not a superkey.

BCNF Decomposition:

  • Manager(ProjectManager, Client)
  • ConsultantAssign(Consultant, ProjectManager)

Exercise 1: Employee Directory

Consider a schema for tracking employee locations:

Employee(EmpID, Name, DeptCode, DeptLocation)

Business Rules:

  • Each employee has one name and belongs to exactly one department.
  • Each department is located in exactly one specific location.

Task: Identify the FDs, evaluate for BCNF compliance, and decompose if necessary.

Exercise 2: Order Processing

Consider a schema for storing line items on customer orders:

OrderDetail(OrderID, ItemID, Quantity, ItemPrice)

Business Rules:

  • An order line specifies a unique quantity for a given order and item combination.
  • Each item has a single, fixed price globally, regardless of which order it belongs to.

Task: Identify the FDs, evaluate for BCNF compliance, and decompose if necessary.

Exercise 3: Semester Transcripts

Consider a schema for tracking university transcripts:

Transcript(StudentID, CourseCode, Semester, Grade)

Business Rules:

  • A student taking a specific course in a specific semester receives exactly one final grade.
  • Grades do not uniquely identify students, courses, or semesters.

Task: Identify the functional dependencies and evaluate if this schema is in 3NF and BCNF. Decompose if necessary.

Exercise 4: Clinic Appointments

Consider a schema for managing medical appointments:

Appointment(ApptID, PatientID, DoctorID, ApptDate, ClinicAddress)

Business Rules:

  • An appointment ID uniquely identifies the patient, the doctor, and the date of the visit.
  • A doctor practices at exactly one specific clinic address.

Task: Identify the FDs, evaluate for BCNF compliance, and decompose if necessary.

Exercise 5: Clinic Shifts

Consider a schema for managing specialized clinic schedules:

ClinicShift(Doctor, ShiftDay, Specialty, Room)

Business Rules:

  • A doctor working on a specific shift day dictates their medical specialty and the room they are assigned to.
  • Each medical specialty is scheduled on exactly one specific day of the week (e.g., Cardiology is only handled on Mondays).
  • Each room is permanently assigned to exactly one dedicated doctor.

Task: Identify the candidate keys and FDs. Verify it is safely in 3NF but not BCNF. Decompose it entirely into BCNF tables.
